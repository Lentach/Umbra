# D2 — Box flood: open band and request queues (design note, 2026-11-17)

Status: DESIGN ONLY. No product code. Prod 0.2.88; Android 0.2.83–0.2.88 in the field.
Authority for the privacy calls: `docs/plans/metadata-privacy-decisions.md` (S8 classes OWNER vs ENGINEERING).

## 1. Threat

The box is anonymous. A `/box` socket needs no account, and the only per-caller limits are per client IP. An attacker with a handful of IPs (a cloud account or a residential proxy pool) can:

- **T1 — fill the open band** (30 000 blobs, 1 GiB of media). Every store that is not into an ESTABLISHED normal queue is then refused with `quota_exceeded`:
  - new friend requests;
  - queue handoffs and re-keys that travel over request queues;
  - sibling swaps;
  - messages into any contact queue less than 7 days old.
- **T2 — fill the WHOLE ceiling** (60 000 blobs, 2 GiB) after waiting out probation. The attacker's own queues become "established" by the attacker's own `subscribe` on day 7. Past that point every send in the box is refused for everyone: a full messaging outage lasting until the junk expires (30 d) or an operator purges it.
- **T3 — silently empty one victim's request queue.** The request sid is served to any account that searches the victim's handle. The queue keeps 50 and drops the oldest, so 50 junk blobs evict every pending friend request and handoff. The sender was answered `ok`, so nobody is told anything.

## 3. Attack map

### What an anonymous client can create and fill

- **Queues.** 120 per IP per 15 min, from any fresh `authPub`. A queue never subscribed is reaped after 24 h with its messages. One signed `subscribe` frame (up to 256 rids, 60 frames per 15 min) keeps up to 1 024 queues per socket alive for 90 d.
- **Blobs.** 1 500 sends per IP per 15 min, into the attacker's own normal queues (128 each, so 235 queues hold 30 000). A blob nobody acks stays 30 d.
- **Media.** 300 uploads per IP per 15 min, each up to 32 MiB, into the attacker's own normal queues. Each queue has a 1 GiB/day budget (`box.constants.ts:85`). Request queues take no media. Media lives 14 d.
- **Other people's request queues.** Any account (registration is rate limited, but free) gets a request sid per `searchUsers`.

### Cost and time to fill

| Goal | Volume | 1 IP | 20 IPs |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 message band (30 000) | 235 queues, 30 000 sends | 2 × 15 min of creates, then 20 send windows ≈ **5 h** | **≈ 15 min** |
| T1 media band (1 GiB) | 32 uploads of 32 MiB | **< 1 min** of quota; bandwidth-bound | — |
| T2 whole ceiling (60 000 / 2 GiB) | 470 queues, minted, subscribed daily, then subscribed again on day 7 | 7 d wait + ≈ 10 h | 7 d wait + ≈ 30 min |
| Keep it full | refill as blobs expire (30 d; media 14 d) | ~1 resend per month | — |
| T3 one victim | 50 sends to a sid served by one search | seconds; repeat while the victim is offline | — |

The per-IP limits are sized for a heavy honest device, not for the fill: **one IP fills the open band in an afternoon.**

### What victims see

- **T1** (band full, established pairs unaffected):
  - a new friend request fails (`quota_exceeded` → "request did not happen", red, retry);
  - a new pair's queue handoff and a friend re-key on a request queue fail and are retried every 24 h (E114c), so new friendships never get a route;
  - messages into contact queues under 7 days old fail;
  - newly linked siblings cannot swap self-queues;
  - media to probation queues gets `429`.
  
  E65b already flags this for queues rotated on a revoke (E6).
- **T2** (whole ceiling): every send and upload is refused. Old clients retry in RAM and show red rows. Everything stays down for 30 d or until an operator acts.
- **T3:** pending friend requests and handoffs vanish with no error on either side. A victim who is online drains the queue as it fills (16-blob window, acks are exempt from the throttle: E64h), so the attack targets an offline victim. There is no request-queue push (decision 2), so "offline" is the normal state.

### What already mitigates

- **Reserved band** (decision 65): it stops T1 from hurting established pairs, but it does NOT stop T2. Since E65c, establishment is something the attacker does to its own queue.
- Per-IP throttles (only linear in the number of IPs). IPv6 /64 widening stops the cheapest rotation.
- Fixed blob size (16 KiB) and the media ladder, so the bytes are bounded.
- An unknown sid stores nothing.
- The 24 h unclaimed reap: an attacker that does not subscribe loses its junk in a day.
- Request queues are kept out of media (T3 cannot spend disk).
- Refusal counts in the logs (`[box] refusals send:ceiling=…`), read by hand. The E4 ceiling-warning item, in flight separately, adds alarms.

## 5. Recommendation

**Now, server-only and safe for every client in the field (one release):**

1. **O1 + O2.** Alarms on band fill (coordinate with E4) and an operator purge command that deletes never-acked, message-heavy queues. Recovery from either T1 or T2 then takes minutes, not 30 days.
2. **O3.** Close the T2 hole: subscribe-alone establishment only for a queue holding nothing (`msgCount = 0`, which keeps E65c's anti-lock-out purpose), establishment otherwise by an ack on/after the probation day, and a 32-blob cap while on probation. T1 then needs 4× the queues, and T2 needs an attacker that runs a send-and-ack loop on each queue for a week. Visible, and O2 purges it.

**Next client release (0.2.9x), flag-gated:**

3. **O7** request-queue friend lane. It protects routes, re-keys and sibling swaps, the part of T3 that breaks friendships silently.

**Design track (changes approved design, owner first):**

4. **O6** blind admission tokens: the only option that makes the fill cost scale with something other than IPs. Ship it optional, then enforce by flag once ≥ 95 % of active devices send tokens. That share can be measured by counting tokened vs untokened sends, with no version reporting.

**Not recommended:**
- O4 alone (it hurts honest bursts for a linear gain).
- O5 (weak against the realistic attacker; costs Android battery).
- O9 for request queues (loses real requests). O9 for probation queues is acceptable but redundant with O3's cap.
- O8: it adds a new per-queue fan-in signal at the box. Revisit only if stranger-request flooding is seen.

## 6. OWNER questions (S8: privacy, limits, what users see, approved-design changes)

1. **Limits.** May an operator purge never-acked, message-heavy queues during an incident (O2), accepting that an honest friend offline since the queue's creation loses those messages?
2. **Limits.** Raise the global ceiling and open band to what the VPS disk allows (today 3 GB of the Postgres disk), with alarms at 50/80 %?
3. **Privacy / approved design (I1).** Changes approved design §3/§4.1. May box admission (queue creation, request sends, probation sends) depend on blind tokens issued per account by the chat server (O6)? The chat server would learn how many tokens each account fetched per day; the box learns nothing.
4. **What users see.** During a band-full incident, should a failed friend request say "servers are busy, try later" instead of today's generic red failure?
5. **Privacy.** O7 lets the box tell "a trusted contact's request blob" from a stranger's on a request queue (not who). Acceptable?
6. **Limits.** Accept the residual that the stranger lane of a request queue stays evictable (T3 for new friend requests) until O6 or O8, given there is no push on request queues (decision 2)?

## 7. Engineering calls this note would make (logged when built)

- O3's exact thresholds: 3 distinct subscribe days, a 32-blob probation cap, and `msgCount = 0` for subscribe-establishment.
- The purge heuristic's ordering (never-acked first, then `msgCount` descending).
- O7's lane split (20 friend / 30 stranger).
